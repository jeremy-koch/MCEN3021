<!-- For a cross-section over which the velocity is constant, the **mass flow rate** is

$$
\dot{m} = \rho U A, \qquad \left[\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{s}}\right] = \left[\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}\right]\left[\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\right]\left[\text{m}^2\right].
$$

The units work, which is the whole reason to believe the formula. The **volumetric flow rate** is just $Q = \dot{m}/\rho$, in m³/s.

Conservation of mass for this duct says whatever goes in must come out — there's nowhere else for it to go:

$$
\dot{m}_1 = \dot{m}_2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \rho U_1 A_1 = \rho U_2 A_2 \quad (\rho = \text{const})
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{\;U_2 = U_1 \frac{A_1}{A_2}\;}
$$

Squeeze the area down by a factor of three and the speed goes up by a factor of three. Nothing subtle, but it is the workhorse. -->

<!--

## Putting them together: how does the pressure change?

Now the actual question. Assume $p_1$ is known — how does pressure change from $A_1$ to $A_2$?

Write Bernoulli between a point at station 1 and a point at station 2 on the same streamline:

$$
p_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\rho U_1^2 + \rho g z_1 \;=\; p_2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\rho U_2^2 + \rho g z_2 .
$$

The duct is horizontal, so $z_1 = z_2$ and those two terms cancel. (More honestly: $z_1 \approx z_2$ — the duct has some height to it, so points across the inlet aren't all at the same elevation. For a duct of this size in water the $\rho g z$ variation is real but small compared to what the velocity terms are doing, and in air it's utterly negligible.)

Substituting $U_2 = U_1 A_1/A_2$ from conservation of mass:

$$
p_2 = p_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho U_1^2\left[1 - \left(\frac{A_1}{A_2}\right)^2\right]
$$

or, written the way you'll more often want it,

::: {.callout-important}
$$
p_1 - p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho U_1^{2}\left[\left(\frac{A_1}{A_2}\right)^{2} - 1\right]
$$
:::

Sanity check the physics before trusting it. For a contraction, $A_1 > A_2$, so the bracket is positive and $p_1 > p_2$: the pressure drops as the flow accelerates into the narrow section. That's the Bernoulli Principle doing exactly what it advertised. And if $A_1 = A_2$ — no contraction at all — the bracket vanishes and $p_1 = p_2$, as it must.

Notice also that the pressure drop scales with the *square* of the area ratio. Halve the area and you don't pay double, you pay $4-1=3$ times the inlet dynamic pressure. This is precisely the effect we will weaponize in the next reading to *measure* flow rate: squeeze the pipe, read the pressure drop, back out the speed. That's a venturi meter.

## Check yourself

<details>
<summary>What is a streamline, in one sentence?</summary>
A line that is everywhere parallel to the velocity vector field. In steady flow it is also the path a fluid parcel actually travels.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Which two forces did we include in ∑F<sub>s</sub> = ma<sub>s</sub>, and which did we throw away?</summary>
We kept gravity and the net force from pressure differences along the streamline. We threw away viscous drag by setting μ ≈ 0.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Where did the V ∂V/∂s on the right-hand side come from?</summary>
The chain rule. The parcel's speed is V(s(t)), so ∂V/∂t = (∂s/∂t)(∂V/∂s) = V ∂V/∂s. A steady flow can still accelerate a parcel, because the parcel moves to where the speed is different.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Why did the parcel's volume disappear from the equation?</summary>
Every term carried a factor of A·Δs (the volume), so it divided out. The result is a statement about the fluid, not about the size of blob we chose to draw.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What does each of p, ½ρV², and ρgz represent physically, and what are their units?</summary>
Flow work, kinetic energy, and potential energy — all per unit volume. The units are J/m³, which is identical to Pa. Every Bernoulli term is simultaneously a pressure and an energy density.
</details>

<details>
<summary>A duct's area doubles. What happens to the speed, and what happens to the pressure?</summary>
U₂ = U₁(A₁/A₂) = U₁/2, so the flow slows down, and by Bernoulli the pressure rises: p₁ − p₂ = ½ρU₁²[(1/2)² − 1] = −(3/8)ρU₁², i.e. p₂ > p₁.
</details>
